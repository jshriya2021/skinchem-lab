01 / Ingredient library

Meet the molecules.

Skincare ingredients are selected for the roles they play in a formulation. Search the mini-library to explore a few well-known examples.

Glycerin

Also known as glycerol, this small, water-attracting molecule is widely used to help keep formulations and the outer skin layer hydrated.

Chemistry note: A triol — it contains three hydroxyl (–OH) groups.

Hyaluronic Acid

A naturally occurring polysaccharide found in the body. In skincare, it is used as a water-binding ingredient.

Chemistry note: A large carbohydrate-based polymer made of repeating units.

Niacinamide

A form of vitamin B3 used in cosmetic formulations. It is studied for several skin-related functions, including supporting the skin barrier.

02 / The pH lab

A little number.
A big chemical idea.

pH is a measure related to the hydrogen-ion activity of a solution. The familiar scale runs from acidic to alkaline, with 7 considered neutral at around room temperature.

Why it matters: The skin surface is mildly acidic, often described around pH 4.5–5.5, although values vary with body site, age, environment and measurement method.

03 / Skin science

Your skin is a living interface.

The outermost layer is not just a covering. It helps limit water loss and acts as a protective barrier between the body and its environment.

The barrier

Cells + lipids

The outer skin layer (stratum corneum) is often compared to a “bricks and mortar” structure: corneocytes are the bricks, while surrounding lipids help fill the spaces between them.

Water balance

Humectants

Humectants such as glycerin attract and hold water. They are used in moisturisers to help improve hydration in the outer skin layer.

Oil + water

Why formulation matters

Water and oil do not naturally mix into a stable, uniform blend. Cosmetic formulators use emulsifiers to help create and stabilise emulsions.

Cleansing

Surfactants

Surfactant molecules have a water-attracting part and an oil-attracting part. This helps them loosen oily soil so it can be rinsed away with water.

04 / Formulation science

From molecules to moisturiser.

A finished cosmetic product is more than a list of ingredients. Formulation chemistry is about how ingredients work together to create a stable, usable product.

MIXTURES
O/W

Emulsions

An emulsion is a mixture of two liquids that normally do not mix, where one is dispersed as droplets in the other. Creams and lotions often use emulsion systems.

Applied chemistry: Interfacial tension, droplet size and emulsion stability.
SURFACE CHEMISTRY
↔

Surfactants

Surfactants can reduce surface or interfacial tension. Their amphiphilic structure gives them both hydrophilic and lipophilic regions.

Applied chemistry: Micelles and cleansing action.
PRODUCT STABILITY
✓

Preservation

Water-containing products can support microbial growth. Appropriate preservation systems help protect product quality when selected and tested correctly.

Applied chemistry: Formulation compatibility and stability testing.
